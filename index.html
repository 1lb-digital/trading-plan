How this works

Six phases. Six gates. You do not move forward until the gate is clear.

A gate is a checkpoint. Each one has a short list of things you must finish. Not "feel ready for." Actually finish, with something written down to prove it. The checkboxes on this page save automatically in your browser, so you can close the tab and come back.

Why gates instead of just a schedule

Almost everyone who loses money trading skipped a step. They learned some patterns, felt confident, and put real money down before they had a written rule they had tested. The gates exist to make that skip impossible.

Read Phase 0 first, even if you skip everything else

Phase 0 is about what AI trading actually is and is not. It is the shortest section and the most important one, because it decides whether the next three months build something real or chase something that was never there.

Phase 0 · Read this first

What AI trading really is

You have seen the videos. Here is what is behind them, and here is the part that is genuinely real.

Start with the regulator

The U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission, the federal agency that oversees futures markets, published a customer advisory about exactly this. Their sentence was: "AI technology can't predict the future or sudden market changes."

That is not a cynic on the internet. That is the agency whose job is watching this market, telling you the marketing does not match reality.

The numbers

  • Roughly 95% of retail bots marketed as "AI" are ordinary rule based scripts running basic moving average or RSI logic, with AI branding added for marketing.
  • Over 80% of retail bot users lose money.
  • Most retail AI trading bots underperform simply buying and holding over any meaningful period.
  • Almost none of the performance claims you see are independently verified. There is no audit, no regulator checking the screenshot.

The scale problem nobody mentions

A single trading strategy at a real quant firm involves a team: researchers with PhDs in statistics or physics, engineers building the data pipeline, traders managing live risk. Dozens of highly paid professionals on one strategy. Those firms run hundreds of researchers and thousands of experiments. Most experiments fail. The ones that work produce small, temporary edges that need constant refreshing.

A thirty dollar a month bot claims to replace all of that. It does not.

The single most important idea in this guide

Writing the bot is the easy 10%. Finding an edge is the hard 90%.

An AI can write you a working trading bot this afternoon. It will connect to a broker, read prices, place orders, and manage stops without you. That problem is solved. What it cannot do is make the bot profitable, because profit comes from the strategy inside it, and the strategy is the part the videos never show.

If the strategy is not proven, automation just executes your losses faster and more reliably than you could by hand.

The five levels of "AI trading"

When someone says AI trading, they could mean any of these. They are wildly different things.

LevelWhat it actually isReal?
Level 1A plain script with "AI" in the product name. Moving averages and RSI. No AI anywhere in it.Marketing only
Level 2A human wrote the rules, AI wrote the code. The logic is entirely yours.This is what you will build
Level 3Machine learning tunes the parameters of a human strategy against historical data.Real, and where overfitting lives
Level 4A model generates the signals itself from engineered features.Real, and institutional work
Level 5An autonomous agent deciding what to trade and why, unsupervised.Mostly does not work at retail

Almost every product sold to retail traders as Level 4 or 5 is actually Level 1. Level 2 is where you can realistically operate, and Level 2 is genuinely powerful, because the hard part becomes something you understand rather than something you rent.

Overfitting, or why the ski slope charts are fake

Learn this word and you can spot most of the fraud

Take any strategy. Test 500 different settings against the same three years of historical data. One of those 500 will show a 400% return with a chart that climbs in a beautiful smooth line.

That setting did not find a pattern in the market. It found the random noise in that specific stretch of history and memorized it. Show it next month's data and it falls apart immediately.

This is overfitting, and it is the mechanism behind nearly every stunning backtest chart in a trading video. The defense is simple and almost nobody does it: test on data you have never looked at. That is Phase 4.

So what is actually real

A research lead at the blockchain analytics firm Glassnode described AI trading tools as working like an assistant or intern who can work 24 hours a day, but who still needs human oversight for any decision that matters. The advice that comes out of the data is to start with AI assisted analysis and execution support, not full autonomy. Use it to remove your weaknesses, not to replace your judgment.

There is also no such thing as set and forget. Even people running successful bots actively monitor and adjust them. In fast moving conditions, a bot left alone for two days can quietly run into its stop loss because the market changed underneath it.

Where AI genuinely helps you, starting today

  1. Backtesting. The big one. AI can write a script that tests your strategy against five years of history in an afternoon. This used to require a programmer. It answers "does this work" in days instead of months. Almost no retail trader does this, and it is the single biggest advantage available to you.
  2. Writing the execution bot once your rules are proven. Easy, real, appropriate.
  3. Analyzing your own trade log for behavioral patterns you cannot see about yourself.
  4. Stress testing your logic before it costs money, by asking it to attack your strategy instead of praise it.
  5. Explaining anything you do not understand, instantly, as many times as you need.

Where AI does not help

Being the thing that decides. Language models are non deterministic, meaning you can ask the same question twice and get different answers. They are slow compared to markets. And they are trained to be agreeable and to find patterns, which is exactly backwards here. Ask an AI "is this a good trade" and it will find you reasons. That is the last thing you want holding the trigger.

The scam checklist

If a product, video, or person hits any of these, close it.

  • Promises passive income or guaranteed returns with no monitoring required
  • Shows a backtest but no verified live track record
  • Profit screenshots with no third party statement behind them
  • Sells you the bot, the course, or the signals rather than trading with their own money
  • Says "AI" and "machine learning" but never explains what the model actually does
  • Pushes you toward a prop firm challenge with an upfront fee
  • Urgency, countdown timers, limited spots
  • The results chart climbs in a smooth line with no drawdowns. Real strategies have ugly stretches.

The honest math on $1,000

Say everything works. Say you build something genuinely good and return 20% in a year, which would beat most professionals. On $1,000 that is $200.

The path from there to life changing money requires either a lot more capital or a lot more leverage. Leverage on a small account is how the account reaches zero, quickly, which is why every rule in this guide fights it.

So here is the honest frame: the prize is not this year's profit. The prize is a tested system and a real skill. If those turn out to be genuine, capital can follow later. That order is not a consolation prize. It is the only order that has ever worked for anybody.

The good news

Everything in this plan makes you a Level 2 operator with a strategy you tested yourself. That puts you ahead of the overwhelming majority of people buying bots, because you will know exactly what yours does and exactly what evidence says it works. Nobody can sell you a black box after this.

The ground rules

Six rules. Read them once now, then again at Gate 5.

  1. No real money until Gate 5 is clear. The $1,000 stays where it is.
  2. All practice happens in a paper account. A paper account is a free practice account that uses real market prices and fake money. It works exactly like the real thing.
  3. Every practice trade gets written down. If it is not in the log, it did not happen. This is not busywork. The log is the entire point of Phase 5.
  4. This is a personal account, not a business account. Trading is not the business's activity, and running it through the business adds paperwork for no benefit. Confirm with the tax preparer before funding anything.
  5. Never add to a losing trade. If a strategy tells you to buy more of something going against you, that strategy is out of scope for this plan. No exceptions. This is the rule that saves accounts.
  6. Risk 1% per trade, maximum. On a $1,000 account that is $10. If a setup needs more risk than that, you skip the setup. You do not stretch the rule to fit the trade.

Read rule 5 again

A strategy that adds to losers will show you an amazing win rate. It will win 9 times out of 10 for months. Then one trade goes far enough against you that the account is gone. This is the single most common way small trading accounts end. If any course, video, or person tells you to average down, close it.

The rules of the game

What $1,000 can actually do

A real regulation stops small accounts from day trading stocks. It decides what you can build.

It is called the Pattern Day Trader rule, or PDT. Plain version: if you use a margin stock account and make 4 or more day trades within 5 business days, your account gets flagged. Once flagged, you need $25,000 in the account to keep going. Below that, you get restricted.

That sounds like it kills the idea at $1,000. It does not, because the rule only applies to one kind of account.

Account typePDT applies?What actually limits you at $1,000
Margin (stocks)Yes4+ day trades in 5 business days flags the account. Under $25,000 you get restricted. Avoid this account type.
Cash (stocks)NoTrade freely, but money from a sale takes one business day to settle before you can use it again. In practice your $1,000 recycles about every other day.
Futures (micro contracts)NoNo trade count limit at all. Micro contracts are small enough for this account size. The real risk is leverage, not regulation.
CryptoNoTrades 24 hours a day, no settlement wait, no trade count limit. The risk is how fast prices move.

So the choice is a cash stock account or a micro futures account. You decide at Gate 2, once you know enough to have an opinion.

One thing to watch for in courses

Some trading courses recommend a broker with very high leverage, trading something called US30 or DJ30 on a platform called MetaTrader. That setup is a CFD, and CFDs are not legal for US retail customers. If a course sends you to an offshore broker, stop and translate it. The legal US way to trade the Dow intraday is Micro E-mini Dow futures, ticker MYM, through a US futures broker.

Prompt · check any broker before you sign up
I am a US resident and I want to open a small trading account with about
$1,000. A course recommended [BROKER NAME] trading [INSTRUMENT] on
[PLATFORM].

Tell me:
1. Is this broker regulated in the US and does it accept US retail clients?
2. Is the instrument a CFD? If yes, explain why US retail clients cannot
   legally use it.
3. What is the closest legal US equivalent instrument and account type?
4. Does the Pattern Day Trader rule apply to that account type?

Phase 3 · Weeks 6 to 7

Write the spec

Course: Day Trading Strategy that can WIN even when you're Wrong by Reid FX. Output: the one document everything else depends on.

8.5 hours62 lecturesRated 4.6Expert level

Why this phase is called "write the spec"

The document you produce here is not homework. It is the specification for your bot. Instrument, timeframe, entry conditions, stop rule, target rule, sizing formula, loss limits. Every one of those is something a program needs to be told.

If you cannot fill in every line, a bot cannot be built, because there is nothing to build. This is the exact point where most people who want automation get stuck without understanding why.

Why this course is last

It is the highest rated of the three and the only one that hands you a single defined strategy instead of a survey. It is also listed as expert level and openly assumes you have been trading for a few months and understand position sizing and leverage. After Phases 1 and 2, you will.

Verify three things before you adopt anything from this course

This is the most important part of this section. Do not skip it.

1. Does the strategy ever add to a losing position?

The title is "WIN even when you're Wrong," and the description talks about managing your way out of most situations and exiting at breakeven or a smaller loss. That can describe two completely different things.

One is legitimate trade management: moving your stop, taking partial profits, cutting early. That is fine.

The other is averaging down: buying more as price goes against you so your breakeven point moves closer. That produces a beautiful win rate and eventually one loss that ends the account.

Watch Section 4, The Strategy, and Section 7, Trade Management, with only this question in mind. If the method ever increases size on a losing trade, stop. Build your strategy from the Moschini breakout method instead. You will not have wasted anything.

2. Can you legally use the recommended setup from the US?

The course teaches US30 and DJ30 on MetaTrader 5 through a forex broker chosen for leverage and spreads. As covered above, that is a CFD setup and US retail clients cannot use it. Plan to translate to Micro E-mini Dow futures, ticker MYM, on a US futures broker. The price levels and logic should carry over. The platform, broker, and sizing math will not.

3. Is there a defined maximum loss per trade?

If the answer is ever "it depends on how the trade develops," that is a red flag. You need a number before you enter, every time. A bot certainly needs one.

Phase 4 · Week 8

Backtest it with AI

This is where AI genuinely changes what is possible for you. Almost no retail trader does this step.

What a backtest is

Running your strategy against years of past price data to see what would have happened, without risking anything and without waiting. If your strategy is garbage, a backtest tells you in an afternoon instead of six weeks.

This used to require hiring a programmer. It does not anymore. That is the real, unhyped version of "AI changes trading," and it is available to you right now.

The rule that makes a backtest honest

Split your history into two pieces before you start. Call them in sample and out of sample.

Example: use 2020 through 2023 to build and adjust. Then do not look at 2024 and 2025 until you are completely done adjusting. When you finally run it on those untouched years, that result is your real answer.

If you tweak the strategy after seeing the out of sample result, that data is burned. You have to split again with new data. This is annoying and it is the whole point. Skipping it is how you overfit.

What to do

  1. Get free historical price data. For stocks, a Python library called yfinance works and costs nothing. For futures or crypto, your broker's API provides it.
  2. Have AI write the backtest script from your Strategy One Pager. You paste the spec, it writes the code.
  3. Run it on your in sample years. Look at the results.
  4. Fix any place where the code does not match what you actually meant. This will happen and it is normal.
  5. Run it once on the out of sample years. Write down the result before you react to it.

You do not need to know how to code

You need to describe your strategy precisely, paste it into an AI, run a file, and read the output. The AI writes the code. Your job is checking that the code does what you meant, which you can do by asking it to explain each part back to you in plain English.

A backtest is evidence, not proof

A good backtest means the strategy is worth testing with your hands. It does not mean it works. Historical data cannot show you slippage, bad fills, or the fact that you will hesitate on the third losing trade in a row. That is why Phase 5 still happens.

And a bad backtest is genuinely great news. It just saved you six weeks and possibly $1,000.

Phase 5 · Weeks 9 to 14

Paper trade it by hand

Six weeks minimum. Forty trades minimum. Still no real money.

Why do this if the backtest passed

Because a backtest tests the strategy. This tests you executing the strategy. Those are different things, and the gap between them is where most people lose money. You will hesitate. You will take a trade that does not qualify. You will move a stop. Better to find that out now.

  • Minimum 40 logged trades before you evaluate anything. Fewer than 40 is noise.
  • Do not change the strategy mid sample. Write the idea down, finish the 40, then test the change as a new version.
  • Trade at the same time of day. Consistency is what makes results readable.

Understanding R

The only number that matters

R means how much you risked on a trade. Risk $10 and make $20, that trade is +2R. Risk $10 and lose it, that is -1R.

Dollar amounts in a paper account are meaningless, because the money is fake. R is not, because it tells you whether the strategy makes more than it loses relative to what you put at risk. Track R. Ignore the fake dollars.

Before you spend a dollar

On taxes

Every single trade is a reportable transaction. Forty trades means forty lines on a tax form. Profits held under a year are taxed at ordinary income rates, not the lower long term rate. There is also a wash sale rule that can disallow losses if you rebuy the same thing within 30 days. None of this is a reason not to trade. It is a reason to tell your tax preparer before you start, not in April.

Phase 6 · Optional · After everything above

Automate it

The thing you wanted from the start. Here is why it comes last, and how to do it without wrecking anything.

You already wrote the bot

Your Strategy One Pager is the specification. Your backtest script already contains the logic. What is left is connecting that logic to a live broker and adding safety rails. That is the smallest part of this project, and it is only small because you did everything else first.

The rule that governs this entire phase

The bot earns its own 40 paper trades before it touches real money. Same standard you had to meet. A bot is a brand new trader with no track record, and the fact that you tested the strategy by hand does not transfer. Code has bugs. Your hands did not.

What a bot needs that a human does not

  1. A kill switch. One command that closes everything and stops trading. Test it before you need it.
  2. A daily loss limit enforced in code. Not in your willpower. The bot stops itself and does not restart until you restart it.
  3. A maximum position check that refuses to place an order bigger than your rules allow, no matter what the logic says.
  4. Logging of every decision, including the trades it decided not to take. When something goes wrong you need to know why.
  5. A heartbeat. Something that tells you it is alive and connected. Silent failure is the worst failure.
  6. An answer for disconnection. What happens if your internet drops with a position open? Decide before it happens.

No set and forget

Even people running successful bots watch and adjust them. A bot left alone for two days in fast conditions can run into its stop loss because the market changed underneath it. Automation removes the emotion, not the responsibility.

Why there is no code on this page

On purpose

Broker APIs change frequently. Anything written today would be out of date by the time you get here, and debugging stale code is worse than writing fresh code. More importantly, the right code depends on whether you chose stocks or futures, which you did not decide until Gate 2.

So instead: the prompts below generate current code against whatever the API looks like on the day you need it. Same approach as every other phase.

Reference

Plain English glossary

Every term in this plan, explained once.

Day trade

Buying and selling the same thing on the same day, so you hold nothing overnight.

Paper trading

A free practice account that uses real market prices and fake money. Everything works the same except nothing is at risk.

Backtest

Running your strategy against past price data to see what would have happened. Fast, free, and easy to fool yourself with.

Overfitting

When a strategy is tuned so tightly to past data that it memorized random noise instead of finding a real pattern. Looks brilliant on old data and falls apart on new data. The cause of most fake results.

In sample and out of sample

Splitting your history in two. You build and adjust on the first part, in sample, and never look at the second part, out of sample, until you are finished. The out of sample result is the honest one.

Lookahead bias

A bug where a backtest accidentally uses information that would not have existed yet at that moment. Makes results look far better than reality.

Position size

How many shares or contracts you buy. Calculated from your stop distance and your risk limit, never from a gut feeling.

Stop (stop loss)

An order that automatically closes your trade at a set price so a loss cannot keep growing. You decide where it goes before you enter.

R

The amount you risked on a trade. Risk $10 and make $20, that is +2R. Risk $10 and lose it, that is -1R. The only fair way to compare trades of different sizes.

Expectancy

The average amount you make or lose per trade, measured in R. Positive expectancy means the strategy makes money over many trades. The number that matters most.

Drawdown

How far your account fell from its highest point before recovering. It tells you how bad the worst stretch felt.

Margin account

A brokerage account that lets you borrow money to trade. The account type the Pattern Day Trader rule applies to.

Cash account

A brokerage account with no borrowing. No Pattern Day Trader rule, but money from a sale takes a day to settle before you can use it again.

Pattern Day Trader (PDT) rule

A US regulation. Make 4 or more day trades in 5 business days in a margin account and you need $25,000 to keep going. Does not apply to cash accounts, futures, or crypto.

Settlement (T+1)

After you sell, the money takes one business day to become usable again. What limits how often a small cash account can trade.

Leverage

Controlling a large position with a small amount of money. Multiplies gains and losses equally. The reason small accounts disappear quickly.

CFD

Contract for Difference. A product that mimics an asset's price without owning it. Common overseas, and not legal for US retail customers.

Micro E-mini futures

Small futures contracts, MYM for the Dow and MES for the S&P 500, designed for smaller accounts. Legal in the US, no PDT rule.

Averaging down

Buying more of something as it moves against you. Makes your win rate look great and is the most common way a small account gets wiped out. Banned in this plan.

Slippage

The gap between the price you expected and the price you actually got. Small but constant, and why live results are worse than paper results.

Wash sale rule

A tax rule. If you sell at a loss and buy the same thing back within 30 days, you may not be able to claim the loss that year.

Breakout

When a price pushes past a level it has been stuck under or above, often leading to a fast move in that direction.

Support and resistance

Price levels where buying or selling has repeatedly stopped a move. Traders use them to decide where to enter and where to put stops.

API

The connection that lets a program talk to your broker directly, placing orders without a human clicking anything. What a bot runs on.

Kill switch

A single command that closes every position and stops the bot immediately. The first thing you build and the first thing you test.
