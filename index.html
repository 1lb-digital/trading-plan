Phase 0 · Read this first

What AI trading is

What the trading videos sell, and what AI can do for you today.

Start with the regulator

The U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission, the federal agency that oversees futures markets, warns in a customer advisory that "AI technology can't predict the future or sudden market changes." (CFTC advisory)

The main idea of this guide

Writing the bot is the easy 10%. Finding an edge is the hard 90%.

AI can write a working trading bot in an afternoon: it connects to a broker, reads prices, places orders and manages stops. Profit comes from the strategy inside the bot, and an untested strategy only loses money faster when a bot runs it.

The five levels of "AI trading"

"AI trading" can mean any of these five things.

LevelWhat it isWhere it fits
Level 1A plain script with "AI" in the product name. Moving averages and RSI, no AI.Marketing only
Level 2A human writes the rules, AI writes the code.Phases 1 to 5 build this
Level 3AI pulls the rules out of trading videos, and testing tunes their settings against history.Phase 6 adds this, and overfitting lives here
Level 4A model generates the signals itself from engineered features.Institutional work
Level 5An autonomous agent deciding what to trade and why, unsupervised.Does not work reliably at retail

Most retail products sold as Level 4 or 5 are Level 1. At Level 2 and 3 you understand every rule your bot follows, because you wrote it or approved it.

Overfitting, or why the ski slope charts are fake

Learn this word and you can spot most of the fraud

Test 500 different settings of any strategy against the same three years of data, and one of them will show a 400% return on a smooth, climbing chart. That setting memorized the random noise in that stretch of history, and it falls apart on next month's data.

This is overfitting, the mechanism behind most stunning backtest charts in trading videos. The defense is to test on data you have never looked at. That is Phase 4.

Where AI helps

  1. Backtesting. AI writes a script that tests your strategy against five years of history in an afternoon, so you learn whether it works in days instead of months.
  2. Reading trading videos for you. AI pulls transcripts and turns a trader's method into written, testable rules (Phase 6).
  3. Writing the execution bot once your rules pass their tests.
  4. Reviewing your trade log for patterns in your own behavior.
  5. Stress testing your logic by attacking your strategy instead of praising it.
  6. Explaining any term as many times as you need.

Where AI should not decide

Language models can give different answers to the same question, run slower than markets, and tend to agree with whoever is asking. Ask one "is this a good trade" and it will find reasons. Keep the model out of the live decision: it writes the rules and the code ahead of time, and the tested code places the trades.

The scam checklist

Close any product, video or person that does any of these:

  • Promises guaranteed returns or passive income with no monitoring
  • Shows a backtest or profit screenshots but no verified live track record
  • Makes its money selling the bot, course or signals rather than trading
  • Says "AI" but never explains what the model does
  • Pushes a prop firm challenge with an upfront fee, or uses countdown timers and limited spots
  • Shows a results chart with no drawdowns. Real strategies have losing stretches.

The math on $1,000

A 20% year, which beats most professionals, makes $200 on $1,000. Leverage is the only way to make more from a small account quickly, and leverage is also how small accounts reach zero, so every rule in this plan limits it. The goal this year is a tested system and the skill to build it. Capital can follow if the evidence holds up.

The ground rules

Read these six rules now and again at Gate 5.

  1. No real money until Gate 5 is clear. The $1,000 stays where it is.
  2. All practice happens in a paper account: a free practice account with real market prices and fake money.
  3. Every practice trade goes in the log. Phase 5 depends on it.
  4. Use a personal account, not a business account. Trading is not the business's activity, and running it through the business adds paperwork. Confirm with the tax preparer before funding anything.
  5. Never add to a losing trade. A strategy that buys more as price moves against you wins 9 times out of 10 for months, then one trade empties the account. Drop any course or video that teaches averaging down.
  6. Risk 1% per trade, maximum. On a $1,000 account that is $10. Skip any setup that needs more.

Account rules

What $1,000 can do

The account type decides how often $1,000 can trade.

The Pattern Day Trader rule (PDT) used to flag any margin stock account that made 4 or more day trades in 5 business days and require $25,000 to keep trading. FINRA replaced it with intraday margin requirements that took effect June 4, 2026, with no $25,000 minimum for day trading. Brokers may keep the old rule until October 20, 2027, so ask yours which rule it applies. Leveraged margin trading still needs $2,000 in equity. (FINRA, checked Oct 30, 2026)

Account typeDay trade limit?What limits you at $1,000
Margin (stocks)Depends on brokerUnder the new rule, no trade count limit, but leverage needs $2,000. A broker still on the old rule restricts accounts under $25,000 after 4 day trades in 5 business days.
Cash (stocks)NoMoney from a sale takes one business day to settle before you can reuse it, so $1,000 recycles about every other day.
Futures (micro contracts)NoNo trade count limit. Micro contracts fit this account size. The risk is leverage.
CryptoNoTrades 24 hours a day with no settlement wait. The risk is how fast prices move.

For $1,000, use a cash stock account or a micro futures account. You decide at Gate 2.

One thing to watch for in courses

Some trading courses recommend a broker with very high leverage, trading something called US30 or DJ30 on a platform called MetaTrader. That setup is a CFD, and CFDs are not legal for US retail customers. If a course sends you to an offshore broker, translate the setup first. The legal US way to trade the Dow intraday is Micro E-mini Dow futures, ticker MYM, through a US futures broker.

Phase 3 · Weeks 6 to 7

Write the spec

Course: Day Trading Strategy that can WIN even when you're Wrong by Reid FX. Output: the one document everything else depends on.

8.5 hours62 lecturesRated 4.6Expert level

Why this phase is called "write the spec"

The document you write here is the specification for your bot: instrument, timeframe, entry conditions, stop rule, target rule, sizing formula and loss limits. A program needs every one of those, so a blank line means there is nothing to build yet. Phase 6 produces the same document from a trader's videos.

Why this course is last

It is the highest rated of the three and the only one that teaches a single defined strategy. It is listed as expert level and assumes you understand position sizing and leverage, which Phases 1 and 2 cover.

Verify three things before you adopt anything from this course

Run the same three checks on any strategy you take from a video in Phase 6.

1. Does the strategy ever add to a losing position?

The title is "WIN even when you're Wrong," and the description talks about managing your way out of most situations and exiting at breakeven or a smaller loss. That can describe two different things.

One is legitimate trade management: moving your stop, taking partial profits, cutting early. That is fine.

The other is averaging down: buying more as price goes against you so your breakeven point moves closer. That produces a beautiful win rate and eventually one loss that ends the account.

Watch Section 4, The Strategy, and Section 7, Trade Management, with only this question in mind. If the method ever increases size on a losing trade, build your strategy from the Moschini breakout method instead.

2. Can you legally use the recommended setup from the US?

The course teaches US30 and DJ30 on MetaTrader 5 through a forex broker chosen for leverage and spreads. That is a CFD setup, which US retail clients cannot use. Plan to translate to Micro E-mini Dow futures, ticker MYM, on a US futures broker. The price levels and logic should carry over. The platform, broker, and sizing math will not.

3. Is there a defined maximum loss per trade?

"It depends on how the trade develops" is a red flag. You and the bot both need a dollar number before every entry.

Phase 4 · Week 8

Backtest it with AI

AI makes this step possible without a programmer, and most retail traders skip it.

What a backtest is

A backtest runs your strategy against years of past price data to show what would have happened. A losing strategy shows up in an afternoon instead of six weeks of paper trading.

The rule that keeps a backtest fair

Split your history into two pieces before you start. Call them in sample and out of sample.

Example: use 2020 through 2023 to build and adjust. Then do not look at 2024 and 2025 until you finish adjusting. When you finally run it on those untouched years, that result is your real answer.

If you change the strategy after seeing the out of sample result, that data is used up and you need a new split with fresh data. Skipping this step is how backtests overfit.

What to do

  1. Get free historical price data. For stocks, a Python library called yfinance works and costs nothing. For futures or crypto, your broker's API provides it.
  2. Have AI write the backtest script from your Strategy One Pager. You paste the spec, it writes the code.
  3. Run it on your in sample years. Look at the results.
  4. Fix every place where the code does not match what you meant. Expect a few.
  5. Run it once on the out of sample years. Write down the result before you react to it.

You do not need to know how to code

You describe the strategy, paste it into an AI, run a file and read the output. To check the code does what you meant, ask the AI to explain each part in plain English.

A backtest is evidence, not proof

A good backtest earns the strategy a paper trading test. Historical data cannot show slippage, bad fills, or how you will act on the third losing trade in a row, so Phase 5 still happens. A bad backtest saves you six weeks and possibly $1,000.

Phase 5 · Weeks 9 to 14

Paper trade it by hand

Six weeks and at least 40 trades, still with no real money.

Why do this if the backtest passed

Because a backtest tests the strategy. This tests you executing the strategy. Most losses come from the gap between the two: hesitating, taking trades that do not qualify, moving stops. Paper trading shows you that gap for free.

  • Minimum 40 logged trades before you evaluate anything. Fewer than 40 is noise.
  • Do not change the strategy mid sample. Write the idea down, finish the 40, then test the change as a new version.
  • Trade at the same time of day so the results are comparable.

Understanding R

The only number that matters

R means how much you risked on a trade. Risk $10 and make $20, that trade is +2R. Risk $10 and lose it, that is -1R.

Paper dollars are fake, but R shows whether the strategy makes more than it loses relative to what it risks. Track R.

On taxes

Every trade is a reportable transaction, so forty trades means forty lines on a tax form. Phase 6 · Paper mode any time · Live money after Gate 5

Build the YouTube-trained agent

An AI agent reads a trader's videos, writes the rules, tests them, and runs them in a paper account.

The risk in one line: most strategies taken from videos fail the backtest, and the gates in this phase catch them before they cost money.

How the pipeline works

  1. Harvest. Pull the transcripts of one trader's strategy videos.
  2. Extract. AI turns the transcripts into a strategy file with exact rules.
  3. Backtest. Code runs the strategy file against years of price data.
  4. Paper trade. A bot runs the same file in a paper account for 40 trades.
  5. Go small. After Gate 5 and the go or no go check, the bot trades real money at 1% risk.

The language model does its work in steps 1 and 2, before the market opens. During market hours, tested code follows the strategy file and places the orders.

Step 2 · Make the rules runnable

The strategy file is the Phase 3 one pager in a format a program reads. Each part maps to code:

  • Signals are values the code calculates from each new price bar, such as the opening range high or a moving average.
  • Entries are conditions that must all be true at the close of a bar.
  • Exits are the stop, the target, and the session end, whichever comes first.
  • Position size is 1% of equity divided by the distance from entry to stop, rounded down. If one share or contract risks more than 1%, the bot skips the trade.
Example · strategy.yaml
name: Opening range breakout (from [VIDEO TITLE], [CHANNEL])
source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=[ID]
instrument: SPY
timeframe: 5m
session: {start: "09:30", stop: "11:30", flatten: "15:55"}   # New York time
signals:
  opening_high: highest high of the first three 5m bars
  opening_low: lowest low of the first three 5m bars
  volume_ok: breakout bar volume > 1.5 x average of the last 20 bars
entry:
  long:  close > opening_high and volume_ok     # all must be true
  short: close < opening_low and volume_ok
stop: opposite side of the opening range
target: 2R
position_size: floor((0.01 * equity) / abs(entry - stop))
max_trades_per_day: 1
max_loss_per_day: 2R
never: add to a losing position
invalidation: out of sample expectancy at or below 0R
undefined: []    # every vague point from the video goes here until decided

Run the three checks from Phase 3 on every strategy file: no adding to losers, a legal US instrument, and a dollar maximum loss per trade. Decide every "undefined" item before the backtest, and write the decision into the file.

How the agent places orders

  1. At each new bar, the bot recalculates the signals from the strategy file.
  2. If every entry condition is true, it calculates the position size from the stop distance.
  3. It checks the limits: trades today, daily loss, maximum position size. Any failed check means no order.
  4. It sends a bracket order, which is the entry with its stop and target attached, so the exit exists at the broker even if the bot disconnects.
  5. It logs the decision, including every trade it skipped and why.
  6. At the session end it cancels open orders and closes any position.

Alpaca's official MCP server lets Claude read a paper account and place orders by chat, and it defaults to paper trading. It suits exploring the account and checking positions. The bot script runs the strategy itself, because the script does the same thing every time.

What the bot needs that a human does not

  • A kill switch: one command that closes everything and stops the bot. Test it first.
  • A daily loss limit in code that stops trading until you restart it.
  • A maximum position check that refuses any order larger than the rules allow.
  • A log of every decision, including skipped trades.
  • A heartbeat that alerts you if the bot stops or loses its connection.
  • A plan for disconnection, which the bracket orders above provide.

Check the bot and its log every trading day.

Reference

Plain English glossary

Every term in this plan, explained once.

Day trade

Buying and selling the same thing on the same day, so you hold nothing overnight.

Paper trading

A free practice account that uses real market prices and fake money. Everything works the same except nothing is at risk.

Backtest

Running your strategy against past price data to see what would have happened. Fast, free, and easy to fool yourself with.

Overfitting

When a strategy is tuned so tightly to past data that it memorized random noise instead of finding a real pattern. Looks brilliant on old data and falls apart on new data. The cause of most fake results.

In sample and out of sample

Splitting your history in two. You build and adjust on the first part, in sample, and never look at the second part, out of sample, until you are finished. The out of sample result is the one to trust.

Lookahead bias

A bug where a backtest accidentally uses information that would not have existed yet at that moment. Makes results look far better than reality.

Position size

How many shares or contracts you buy. Calculated from your stop distance and your risk limit, never from a gut feeling.

Stop (stop loss)

An order that automatically closes your trade at a set price so a loss cannot keep growing. You decide where it goes before you enter.

R

The amount you risked on a trade. Risk $10 and make $20, that is +2R. Risk $10 and lose it, that is -1R. The only fair way to compare trades of different sizes.

Expectancy

The average amount you make or lose per trade, measured in R. Positive expectancy means the strategy makes money over many trades. The number that matters most.

Drawdown

How far your account fell from its highest point before recovering. It tells you how bad the worst stretch felt.

Margin account

A brokerage account that lets you borrow money to trade. The account type day trading margin rules apply to.

Cash account

A brokerage account with no borrowing. No day trade limit, but money from a sale takes a day to settle before you can reuse it.

Pattern Day Trader (PDT) rule

The old FINRA rule: 4 or more day trades in 5 business days in a margin account required $25,000. Replaced by intraday margin requirements on June 4, 2026; brokers may keep the old rule until October 20, 2027. Never applied to cash accounts, futures or crypto.

Settlement (T+1)

After you sell, the money takes one business day to become usable again. What limits how often a small cash account can trade.

Leverage

Controlling a large position with a small amount of money. Multiplies gains and losses equally. The reason small accounts disappear quickly.

CFD

Contract for Difference. A product that mimics an asset's price without owning it. Common overseas, and not legal for US retail customers.

Micro E-mini futures

Small futures contracts, MYM for the Dow and MES for the S&P 500, designed for smaller accounts. Legal in the US, with no day trade limit.

Averaging down

Buying more of something as it moves against you. Makes your win rate look great and is the most common way a small account gets wiped out. Banned in this plan.

Slippage

The gap between the price you expected and the price you got. Small but constant, and why live results are worse than paper results.

Wash sale rule

A tax rule. If you sell at a loss and buy the same thing back within 30 days, you may not be able to claim the loss that year.

Breakout

When a price pushes past a level it has been stuck under or above, often leading to a fast move in that direction.

Support and resistance

Price levels where buying or selling has repeatedly stopped a move. Traders use them to decide where to enter and where to put stops.

API

The connection that lets a program talk to your broker directly, placing orders without a human clicking anything. What a bot runs on.

Kill switch

A single command that closes every position and stops the bot immediately. The first thing you build and the first thing you test.
